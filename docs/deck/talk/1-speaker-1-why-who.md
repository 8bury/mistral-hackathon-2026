# Speaker 1: Why + Who (slides 1–4, ~85 s)

You open the pitch. Your job: make the room feel the problem, then show who we built for.
**Order:** **Speaker 1** → Speaker 2 → Demo → Speaker 3.
Present from `docs/deck/index.html` (→ / Space = next).

---

## Slide 1 · Title
"Hi, we're [team]. This is **Vysta**: the city that answers back.
You point your phone at something, ask out loud, and it shows you exactly what it's talking about."

## Slide 2 · The moment
"Picture a Saturday in the City of London. You stop in front of a dragon on a pillar and think: *what is that?*
The guidebook has 400 pages, and none of them are about this.
The audio tour is talking about something two streets away.
A photo search gives you a text card, but doesn't tell you which part of the building it means.
So you give up, and the moment is gone."

*Pause after "the moment is gone".*

## Slide 3 · The gap
"Look at the tools we have. Some let you ask, some answer out loud, some understand what's in front of you.
**None of them point.**
Vysta does all four: you ask by voice, it answers by voice, about what you're looking at, with the exact spot highlighted."

## Slide 4 · Personas
"And everyone wants something different. Same dragon, four travellers:
- An eight-year-old wants wonder: our **Little Soldier** guide.
- An architecture student wants materials and intent: **The Architect**.
- A history buff wants patrons and dates: **The Historian**.
- A visitor from abroad wants it in their own language, so the guides speak Spanish too.

And if none of those fit, there's a fourth guide, **You**: you write the style you want yourself.
Same facts, retold for each person."

## Q&A you own
- **"Who is it for?"** Tourists and families in cities, plus anyone who wants depth: students and history fans.
- **"Why not just use Google Lens / an audio guide?"** They answer, but they don't point at the exact spot, and they don't let you just ask out loud about what's in front of you.
